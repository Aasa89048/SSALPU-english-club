LET'S GET THE IDEA

What is the Present Simple?

Think of it as your everyday tense. We use the Present Simple to talk about routines, things that are generally true, and situations that stay the same.

☀
The short version

If something happens regularly or is true most of the time, the Present Simple is probably your tense.

How to build a sentence

NICE AND SIMPLE
I / YOU / WE / THEY+base verbI work here.
HE / SHE / IT+verb + sShe works here.
i

Little detail, big difference: Add -s or -es to the verb with he, she, or it. He reads. She watches. It works.

Make it negative or ask a question

WITH DO / DOES

Don't / doesn't + base verb
ASK A QUESTION

Form, meaning, and use

The Present Simple is a present-tense form used to describe situations viewed as stable, repeated, or generally valid. The speaker presents the situation as a fact, habit, state, or timetable—not as an activity unfolding at this moment.

Core uses

  • Habits and repeated events: I walk to work every morning.
  • General truths: Water boils at 100°C at sea level.
  • States and opinions: She owns a small bookshop. I agree.
  • Timetables and programmes: The train leaves at 8:15 tomorrow.

Third-person spelling

  • Most verbs take -s: read → reads.
  • Verbs ending in -ch, -sh, -ss, -x, or -o usually take -es: watch → watches; go → goes.
  • Consonant + y changes to -ies: study → studies. Vowel + y takes -s: play → plays.
  • Have becomes has; be has the forms am, is, and are.

Adverbs of frequency

Frequency adverbs usually come before the main verb: “They often visit us.” With be, they usually follow the verb: “She is usually early.”

Common adverbs include always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, and never. Their position can change for emphasis.

Present Simple or Present Continuous?

Use the Present Simple for a routine or stable fact: “He works at a hospital.”

Use the Present Continuous for an activity happening now or a temporary situation: “He is working from home this week.”

Some state verbs, such as know, believe, own, and prefer, are not normally used in continuous forms.

A FEW FRIENDLY REMINDERS

Little tips that stick

01
Watch out for he, she, and it.

That little -s matters! Say “Maya walks,” not “Maya walk.”

02
After “does,” drop the -s.

“Does he work here?” and “He doesn't like tea.” The -s is already in does, so keep the next verb simple.

03
Listen for routine words.

Always, usually, often, sometimes, and every day are big clues that the Present Simple fits.

04
Use it for “state” verbs, too.

For thoughts, feelings, and preferences, say “I know,” “she likes it,” or “they need help.”
